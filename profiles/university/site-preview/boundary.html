How a self holds together

A self is not a thing you have. It is an edge you keep up: between you and everything else, paid for with energy, and kept useful by guessing what comes next. See how that edge works and a lot of ordinary life makes sense, from a good week to a week when the world shrinks to the next hour.

Young adultsEveryday life

By the end you will be able to...
  1. See a self as an edge that has to be paid for.
  2. Say why what you can sense and plan for grows and shrinks with what you have to spend.
  3. Tell a world that is short from a world that is unsteady, and what each one costs.
  4. Say what a group gains and what it risks when its members share signals and food.
1

An edge that has to be paid for

Why you need this: How far ahead you can see and plan is not fixed. It grows and shrinks with what you have to spend, and with how well your guesses about the world hold up.

Lab · if this, then thatThe edge

This is one small self. The dashed ring is how far out it can sense. Gold is food, pink is harm, and it has one gate to meet them with. Choose its world, guess what its edge will do, then watch.

the dashed ring = your boundary: how far out you can sense and carea dashed line = a predictiona white flash = the gap between your prediction and what happenedthe gate = an action: you can meet one thing at a timethe gold inside = the energy that pays for all of it

A model of Bryan Korth's boundary, predict, act, aware loop. The reach is drawn after Michael Levin's idea of a cognitive light cone. It is a model to think with.

You will be able to: See a self as an edge that costs energy to hold, and say what makes it grow or pull in.

Why it matters

Every living thing, from one cell to a person to a town, keeps an inside apart from an outside. Keeping that edge up takes energy. What the edge lets it do is sense what is coming, guess where it will land, and meet it. When the guesses hold, more comes in than the edge costs, and it can afford to reach further. When they fail, or there is too little to go round, the edge pulls in.

How this reaches you

A week with enough sleep and enough money, you can think about next year. A week with neither, you can only deal with what is in front of you. That is not a flaw in you. It is the edge being paid for with less.

Do this week

Notice one day this week when your world felt small and only the next hour mattered. Ask what was short that day: sleep, money, time, or a world that kept changing on you.

Who teaches this unit

  • Bryan Korth, builder of this model. Worked out the boundary, predict, act, aware loop over years of his own notes, and built Void University.
  • Michael Levin, biologist at tufts university. Showed that groups of cells use electrical signals to agree on what body to build and rebuild, and gave the name cognitive light cone to how far in space and time a living thing can pursue a goal.
  • Karl Friston, neuroscientist at university college london. Proposed the free energy principle: that living things stay alive by keeping the gap between what they predict and what they sense small.
2

Many small selves, one larger self

Why you need this: Every group you belong to works this way: a body, a family, a crew, a town. Joining changes who gets warned, who gets fed, and who goes down when there is not enough.

Lab · if this, then thatSeven together

Seven small selves, each with its own reach, its own gate and its own energy. The one in the middle is walled in by the others and can never feed itself. Choose whether they are joined, guess how many will still be funded at the end, then watch.

a line between two = a join: signals and food pass along itthe joins lighting up = one sensed it, now all knowthe middle one = a member that cannot feed itselfa dark one = its funding stopped, so it stopped

The same loop as The edge, one scale up, from Bryan Korth's framework. The join follows Michael Levin's work on cells that share signals through gap junctions. It is a model to think with.

More: why it matters, what else to watch, read and do, who teaches it
You will be able to: Say what changes, and what does not, when small selves join up.

Why it matters

Nothing new is added when small selves become a larger one. Each still pays for its own edge and makes its own predictions. What changes is the join between neighbours: what one senses, all know, and food flows from the fuller to the emptier. That is enough to keep alive a member that could never feed itself. It is also enough to tie every member to the same fate.

How this reaches you

Your own body is this. Most of your cells never meet food; they are carried by the ones that do. So is a household with one income, and a team where one person hears the bad news first.

Do this week

Pick one group you are part of. Ask what passes along its joins: who hears news first, and who is carried. Then ask what would happen to each of you if the joins were cut.
