# The mind's mechanisms: draft list for Bryan

Bryan, 15 Nov 2026: "sunk cost fallacies, cognitive dissonance et cetera ... people don't know how these affect
things. These mechanisms are so vital for so much." Each one gets an everyday one-to-one analogy, a moving scene
(scene.js) and one line on how it costs you. Every line here is a draft for Bryan to keep, swap or strike.
All 18 are built (15 Nov 2026) as units of courses/minds-mechanisms.json; the analogies there are the current ones.

| Mechanism | Everyday analogy | The one-to-one | How it costs you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunk cost | Sitting through a bad movie because you paid for the ticket | the ticket = money already spent; leaving = the choice still open | You keep paying (time, money, years) for what is already lost |
| Cognitive dissonance | Wearing shoes that hurt, then telling everyone they are comfy | the pinch = the fact; "they're comfy" = the story you tell to stop the pinch | You defend a bad choice instead of fixing it |
| Fear of missing out | The door closing on a crowded elevator, so you jump in | the closing door = "only today"; the jump = buying before thinking | Rushed buys, bad deals, scrolling all night |
| Confirmation bias | Searching your house only where you hope the keys are | the rooms you skip = the evidence you don't look at | You collect only what agrees with you |
| Anchoring | A shirt "was $90, now $45" | the $90 = the first number you hear; it sets what feels cheap | You judge prices and pay by someone else's starting number |
| Loss aversion | Dropping a $20 bill hurts more than finding one feels good | a loss weighs about twice a gain | You hold bad stocks, avoid good risks |
| Availability | Being scared of sharks but not of the drive to the beach | what you remember easily = what feels likely | You fear the wrong things and miss the real ones |
| Bandwagon | Picking the busy restaurant over the empty one | the line outside = "everyone thinks so" | You follow the crowd off a cliff (fads, bubbles) |
| Status quo bias | Never changing your phone plan though a cheaper one exists | the default = what you already have | You overpay for staying put |
| Framing | "90% fat free" vs "10% fat" on the same yogurt | the wrapper = the words; the yogurt = the facts | The same thing sells or scares depending on the wrapper |
| Reciprocity | The free sample at the store, then you feel you should buy | the sample = the small gift; the buy = the debt it creates | Free things that cost you more |
| Authority | Doing what a man in a lab coat says in an ad | the coat = the costume of knowing | You trust the uniform, not the evidence |
| Scarcity | "Only 2 left in stock" | the number = pressure, not fact | You want it more because it seems rare |
| Hindsight bias | "I knew it" after the game is over | the final score = what makes the past look obvious | You think you can predict, and bet like it |
| Dunning-Kruger | A new driver who thinks they drive great | knowing a little = not knowing what you don't know | Confident mistakes |
| Planning fallacy | "This will take an hour" for a job that takes all day | the hour = the plan with nothing going wrong | Late, over budget, every time |
| Variable reward (the slot machine) | A slot machine, and the pull-to-refresh on your phone | the lever = refresh; the payout = a new like or post | Hours gone to feeds built on this |
| Halo effect | Trusting a good-looking salesperson more | one bright feature lights up everything else | You judge the whole by one part |

Each scene, like the bill and the receipt: act out the analogy, then the analogy's labels turn into the
mechanism's labels in place. These connect to Bryan's entry 3 (IFTTT and the mechanisms) and to the Mind domain.
