# Human nature: course map from Yale PHIL 181 (draft for Bryan)

Bryan chose this course on 15 Nov 2026, as the first found course Void University turns into its own curriculum:
"it's not just we show videos we create curriculum from what we find".

**Source:** Yale PHIL 181, *Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature*, taught by Tamar Gendler and recorded in
spring 2011. Open Yale Courses publishes it under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, so what we build from it carries the same licence
and credits Yale and Professor Gendler.

**What Yale gives us for each lecture** (oyc.yale.edu/philosophy/phil-181/lecture-N):
- an overview
- timestamped chapters
- the full transcript
- the slides (PDF)
- a reading guide (PDF)

**Video:** all 26 lectures are on the Internet Archive, in the item `philosophy-and-the-science-of-human-nature`
(numbered files, 42 to 50 minutes each).

**What we add to each unit:**
1. An everyday analogy scene, like the other courses.
2. The lecture video, split into its chapters so people can watch it in pieces.
3. **Our quiz:** about five questions written from the transcript. Each answer quotes the line it comes from,
   with the timestamp, so a checker can confirm every answer against the source.
4. The readings. The original texts are public domain, so we use free copies (Gutenberg and the Archive):
   - Plato's *Republic* (Jowett)
   - Aristotle's *Ethics*
   - Epictetus's *Handbook*
   - Hobbes's *Leviathan*
   - Mill's *Utilitarianism*
   - Kant's *Groundwork*

   The modern books on the syllabus (Haidt, Hackett translations) get a library link, never an upload. These old
   texts are also the first test of the plain-English shelf: the original beside a modern rewrite.
5. Links to the mind's mechanisms where a lecture uses one (lecture 11, for example, links to the planning fallacy
   and to variable reward).
